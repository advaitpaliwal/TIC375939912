# A 2.1 Earth-radius Planet Candidate on a 4.30-day Orbit around the K Dwarf TIC 375939912

Advait Paliwal
Independent researcher, Sterling Heights, MI 48314, USA
advaitpaliwal@gmail.com

## Abstract

I report a transit-like signal on TIC 375939912 (TYC 525-1773-1), a K dwarf at 84.5 pc, found in TESS full-frame-image photometry from Sectors 55 and 82. A transit fit gives a period of 4.300398 ± 0.000038 d, a depth of 868 ± 76 ppm and a duration of 2.43 h, which implies a radius of 2.06 ± 0.18 R⊕. The star is not a TOI or CTOI. The signal is present in raw and corrected photometry, and the TESS-SPOC and QLP light curves agree on its depth. Difference images exclude all 23 Gaia sources within 60″ as the source. With these exclusions and Gaia companion limits, TRICERATOPS gives FPP = 0.0075 ± 0.0019 over ten runs, below the 0.015 validation threshold. No high-resolution imaging exists, so I present it as a strong candidate, not a validated planet.

## Star and search

TIC 375939912 (Stassun et al. 2019) has T = 10.58, Teff = 4635 ± 121 K, R★ = 0.708 ± 0.052 R☉, M★ = 0.74 ± 0.09 M☉ and Gaia DR3 RUWE 0.98 (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023). ExoFOP lists no imaging, spectroscopy or radial-velocity follow-up. The star was observed only in full-frame images, in Sector 55 at 600 s and Sector 82 at 200 s. I searched the TESS-SPOC light curves (Caldwell et al. 2020) of 2,220 K and M dwarfs within 100 pc that have exactly two full-frame sectors and no 2-minute data, using Transit Least Squares (Hippke & Heller 2019). Each detection had to pass three gates before a false-positive analysis: the dip must appear in raw as well as corrected flux, the two pipelines must agree on its depth, and difference images must place it on the target.

## Transit fit

I fitted the TESS-SPOC data with batman (Kreidberg 2015) and emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013), after detrending with wotan (Hippke et al. 2019). The model integrates over each sector's exposure time, assumes a circular orbit, and uses a TIC stellar-density prior and quadratic limb darkening from Claret (2017). It gives P = 4.3003975 (+0.0000380/−0.0000386) d, T0 = 2460542.8170 (+0.0040/−0.0046) BJD_TDB, Rp/R★ = 0.0268 ± 0.0013, a/R★ = 13.4 (+1.0/−1.2), b = 0.22 (+0.20/−0.15), a depth of 868 (+75/−76) ppm and T14 = 2.43 (+0.20/−0.16) h (Figure 1). This implies Rp = 2.06 (+0.19/−0.18) R⊕, a = 0.0468 ± 0.0019 AU, an insolation of 95 (+9/−8) S⊕ and Teq = 897 (+48/−41) K.

## Vetting

Using event-local baselines, raw and corrected fluxes give consistent depths, so the signal is not created by the pipeline correction. Per-sector depths are 956 ± 117 and 769 ± 92 ppm in TESS-SPOC, and 919 ± 113 and 903 ± 106 ppm in the independent QLP light curves (Huang et al. 2020a, 2020b; Kunimoto et al. 2021). Injected 770 ppm transits are recovered at 673–840 ppm in all four products. Odd and even depths differ by 0.48σ in my own detrending and by 0.73σ in QLP. The pipeline-detrended TESS-SPOC light curve shows 2.3σ, with the sign reversing between sectors and pipelines. There is no secondary eclipse at phase 0.5 (−38 ± 77 ppm). The transit-derived stellar density, 2.5 ± 0.6 g cm⁻³, matches the TIC value of 2.9 ± 0.8 g cm⁻³.

I made difference images from TESScut cutouts (Brasseur et al. 2019) and fitted the in-transit flux deficit with the TESS PRF at the target and at every Gaia source within 60″. All 23 neighbours are disfavoured relative to the target by a summed Δχ² of at least 29.2, and none is preferred in either sector. The nearest is a G = 21.0 star 10.8″ away.

## False-positive probability

TRICERATOPS (Giacalone & Dressing 2020; Giacalone et al. 2021), averaged over ten runs of 10⁶ draws each, gives FPP = 0.049 ± 0.002 and NFPP = 0.0069 without constraints. Removing the 23 excluded neighbours sets NFPP to zero for catalogued sources. Adding companion limits from Gaia astrometry and imaging via MOLUSC (Wood et al. 2021) in the same runs gives FPP = 0.0075 ± 0.0019 (range 0.0048–0.0109). All ten runs fall below the 0.015 threshold. Modelling the exposure time as 600 s or 200 s instead of 30 minutes gives 0.0068–0.0072. Almost all of the remaining probability is a planet orbiting an unresolved bound companion. Gaia cannot exclude companions inside about 1 AU. Collier et al. (2026) find that small planets validated with TRICERATOPS usually needed a contrast curve, and this star has no high-resolution imaging, so I present it as a strong candidate pending imaging.

## Summary

TIC 375939912.01 is a 2.1 R⊕ candidate with an equilibrium temperature of about 900 K, around a bright K dwarf. TESS observed it in only two sectors, and no further coverage is predicted through Sector 134. High-resolution imaging and ground-based photometry are the most useful next steps. SIMBAD links the star to two studies of stellar groupings (Coronado et al. 2022; Moranta et al. 2022); if it is a member of one, its age could be constrained. Code and data are at https://github.com/advaitpaliwal/TIC375939912.
